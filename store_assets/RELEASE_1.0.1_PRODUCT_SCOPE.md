# ChoreJar 1.0.1 product scope and store declarations

## What ChoreJar is

ChoreJar is a parent-managed family organizer for chores, allowance records,
savings goals, and a private history of entries that the parent records.
Parents set up the family, approve completed chores, and decide whether to
record money given, savings moves, or expenses.

Child profiles do not require an email address, password, public profile, or
direct-contact feature. A linked child can see their own tasks, send a task
for review, choose a savings-goal picture and name, and follow progress toward
that goal.

## What ChoreJar is not

ChoreJar does not transfer, hold, process, send, withdraw, cash out, lend, or
pay money. It does not provide a wallet, payment account, bank account,
financial product, cash reward, or child-initiated payout. Any real-world
decision to give money or buy an item happens outside the app and is made by a
parent or guardian.

## Child and advertising boundary

The child experience has no advertising and no money-moving controls. The free
parent experience may show one age-restricted, non-personalized,
family-safe banner after an adult confirms they are entering the parent area.
The app configures the Google Mobile Ads SDK for child treatment and G-rated
ad content. No family names, chores, goals, balances, or money records are
sent to advertising. Store declarations must continue to disclose the SDK data
practices that apply to the production binary.

Rewarded advertising and paid subscriptions are intentionally not enabled in
version 1.0.1. Their ad units and proposed prices are retained only as account
configuration history, not as a shipped product feature or store claim.

## Store-review wording

Use this explanation wherever a store asks about money, rewards, or child
functionality:

> ChoreJar is a household organizer and record keeper. A parent manually
> records a chore reward, allowance, money given, savings move, or expense only
> after the real-world event. The service never transfers, stores, or pays out
> funds. A child cannot request, withdraw, cash out, approve, send, or spend
> money in the app.

## Reviewer demonstration path

1. Create a parent account with any reviewer email.
2. Add a child profile and a task with a reward amount.
3. Approve the submitted task, then record money given from the parent view.
4. Open the child view and observe task, balance categories, and savings-goal
   progress.
5. On the child goal screen, the app says to discuss real-world choices with a
   parent; it offers no payout or money-request control.
